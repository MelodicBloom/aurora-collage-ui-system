A practical audit for teams who want clearer buying decisions without urgency theater. Use it on a storefront, service page, portfolio, donation flow, booking flow, or any interface that asks a person to decide.

12 decision-friction cards

01 / NEXT ACTIONCan I tell what happens next?One dominant action; secondary actions visually quieter. Test at 360px and with keyboard only.
02 / CONFIDENCEWhat evidence is missing?Show scale, material, detail, availability, shipping, returns, or process evidence before adding persuasion.
03 / STATEDid the interface acknowledge me?Pressed, selected, pending, success, error, and disabled states must be visibly distinct and semantically exposed.
04 / COSTWhat surprise appears late?Surface price, shipping threshold, commitments, and exclusions before the person crosses a point of no return.
05 / RECOVERYCan I change my mind?Quantity, removal, undo, edit, back, and cancel paths should not feel punitive.
06 / MOBILEWhat collapses badly?Check tap targets, sticky regions, clipping, reading order, drawer behavior, and meaningful content priority.
07 / MOTIONDoes motion explain state?If animation does not clarify cause, continuity, hierarchy, or completion, remove it or make it quieter.
08 / ACCESSCan I finish without a pointer?Tab through the entire decision path. Visible focus and equivalent outcomes are non-negotiable.
09 / LANGUAGEIs this precise or merely persuasive?Replace vague reassurance with concrete facts, boundaries, and what the person receives.
10 / TRUSTWhich claim is actually measured?Label hypotheses, implementation evidence, instrumented events, and measured outcomes separately.
11 / SHAREIs there something worth passing on?Create a useful artifact, reference, saved configuration, or proof link before asking for a share.
12 / LEADDid value arrive before capture?Offer the useful thing first. Make follow-up optional and contextual rather than a toll booth.

One-page measurement rule

Never write “improves conversion” into the component contract. Write: buyer need → mechanism hypothesis → observable event → success metric → guardrail → evidence status. A result becomes “measured” only when the instrumentation and source receipt exist.
